# ASD-STE100 — what this template may take from it, and where it helps

**Written**: 14/10/2026 · **Driver**: Sam's question (14/10/2026) — where can ASD-STE100 help
the project's writing without making it too long? · **Feeds**: `MAP-SENTENCE-RULES.md` N-001,
and through it slices S-01 and S-02

## Question

Can this MIT Copier template adopt ASD-STE100 Simplified Technical English, or derive rules from
it, and on which kinds of text does the evidence say it helps?

## Verdict

**Read it and cite it. Never derive from it, ship it, or name the house rules after it.** Issue 9
is all-rights-reserved: free to download, but its reproduction right covers eight named classes
of organisation, and a template that ships its text into every generated project is in none of
them. No conformance scheme for documents is described, so "STE-compliant" cannot be claimed truthfully without the
full rule set and the dictionary, and the dictionary does not fit developer vocabulary anyway.

**What survives is the idea, not the text.** The sentence-level ideas (one action per step,
condition first, short procedural sentences, the warning before the command) are not protected
by copyright and are shared by openly licensed guidance. The evidence for them is about
**human readers of difficult or complex procedures, especially non-native ones**. There is no
evidence for LLM readers, and neural machine translation gains little from controlled-language
rules.

**Not legal advice.** Every licence position below is read from the source's own terms.

**What follows for this template.** Naming the standard accurately is ordinary reference. Calling
the house rules "STE", "Simplified Technical English" or a confusable variant is not: the risk is
confusion and implied endorsement, which ASD forbids, more than infringement of a figurative
mark. Neither is any claim of compliance. A client contract that requires STE (ATA iSpec 2200,
or S1000D, which recommends it — [About STE](https://asd-ste100.org/about_STE.html)) means that
project obtains the standard and a checker itself.

| Across five MT systems, only four of nine controlled-language rules helped individually. For neural MT, rules that had helped older systems did not have the same effect, and neural MT gave the best output with or without them                                                                                                                                                       | [Marzouk (2021), Machine Translation 35](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10590-021-09266-0)                                                                 | Moderate; German, not STE      |
| Of 21 input rewrites tested (English into six languages, three open-weight LLMs), simplification was the most effective MT-agnostic one, and human evaluation found meaning largely kept                                                                                                                                                                                                | [Ki and Carpuat (2025), NAACL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.16682)                                                                                     | Moderate; not STE-specific     |
| **No primary measurement** shows whether STE makes text longer or shorter. STEMG's "shorter" claim cites no data                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        | [STE FAQ](https://asd-ste100.org/STE_faq.html)                                                                                                       | None                           |
| **No primary evidence** that controlled language helps an LLM read instructions or tool descriptions. Studies of MCP tool descriptions find that **missing** purpose and parameter semantics hurt tool selection; fully expanded descriptions trade higher success for many more steps, and compact ones keeping the key components often do as well. Neither tests controlled language | [arXiv 2602.14878](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14878) · [arXiv 2602.18914](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.18914)                                          | No test of STE exists          |

**Consequence.** Justify any rule taken here by **human** readers of difficult procedures,
especially non-native ones. Put no word caps on Claude-facing text, and measure any claimed benefit for a model reader before
stating it — the standard the README already applies to the ICM paper.

Copying a sentence from either of the first two would carry an attribution notice into every
generated project. The house precedent is to re-author from ideas and measure five-gram overlap,
as was done for TigerStyle.

## 5. Tooling

ASD endorses no checker. It says not every rule can be checked automatically, and names sentence
length, long multi-word nouns and passive voice as the checks a tool should manage. The commercial
checkers (HyperSTE, Congree) target XML authoring stacks, not Markdown under git. Vale's official
package library has no STE style; community Vale styles and STE linters exist, the earliest from
August 2025 and most from mid-2026, and none has been validated. A small in-repo audit covering
the structural checks is the honest scope ([STE software](https://asd-ste100.org/STEsoftware.html)
· [Vale packages](https://github.com/vale-cli/packages) ·
[one community style](https://github.com/aldair-torres/ste100-vale-rules)).
